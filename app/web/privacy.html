Privacy

Last updated: 26 September 2026

Friends is a small app for planning things to do with your friend groups. This instance is run by a private person on their own server. It isn't a company product: there are no ads, no tracking and no analytics, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone.

What is stored

  • Your account: your email address, display name and password (stored only as an Argon2 hash), and your timezone. Optionally, a picture link and your birthday: day and month, plus a year that only you ever see.
  • What you and your groups create: groups and invites, ideas in the backlog and their details, categories, polls and votes, "interested" marks, calendar events, and wheel spins. Each group also keeps a history of changes (who did what, and when), for the group's own recaps.
  • Sessions: sign-in tokens (stored hashed), the kind of device ("android", "ios" or "web") and your last sign-in time.
  • Server logs: for each request, the address it came from, your user ID and the path requested. Passwords and tokens are never logged.

Who sees it

  • Members of a group see that group's content, and each other's names and pictures.
  • Your birthday (day and month only) shows in the calendar of each group you're in. You can turn that off per group, in the group's "Group" tab.
  • The person running the server can technically access the database, as with any self-hosted service.

Where it is kept

On the server behind this address, with nightly backups kept for about two weeks. Opening a link or an address in Friends hands it to your browser or maps app, which follow their own policies.

Deleting your data

Go to Profile → Delete account in the app. Your account is anonymised right away: your email address, name, picture and birthday are removed, and so are your votes and "interested" marks. You are signed out everywhere, and any group you owned passes to another member. What you created in a group, such as ideas, events and polls, stays there for the others, shown as "Deleted user". Backups age out within about two weeks.

Contact

Questions or requests: ask the person who invited you, or the owner of this instance.
